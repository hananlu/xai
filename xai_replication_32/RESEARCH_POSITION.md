# Research position after the new-cohort main evaluation

The main evaluation, prespecified density/uncertainty sensitivities, independent audits and real-kernel notebook execution are complete. Read all fixed estimates and valid-draw counts in RESULTS_BRIEF.md and SENSITIVITY_RESULTS.json.

## What the evidence supports

The original eight-target frozen-GRU h1 source-calendar contrast was -0.0189 with nominal 95% interval [-0.0891, 0.0533], seven complete pairs. The new 32-target cohort yields -0.007535 with nominal interval [-0.040153, 0.024125], 30 complete pairs and 1,885 valid of 2,000 attempted original pair/block draws. Both intervals contain zero. No equivalence margin was specified. Neither result establishes systematic degradation or equivalence.

All twelve fixed model/horizon/reference Shapley contrasts in the new cohort have nominal intervals containing zero. The original h1 history-median secondary negative intervals therefore do not recur as zero-excluding intervals here. Reporting only the original stronger-looking reference contrast would misrepresent the available evidence.

Forecast behavior is heterogeneous. Target 267 has a 2024 weekly MASE scale of approximately 751 bytes. Frozen GRU seed 2026 h6 produces about 4.445 MB MAE and MASE about 5,921 in 2025. The original mean MASE remains unchanged; a post-hoc all-cell scale plot and medians explain the extreme ratios. This is a real relative forecasting failure on a nearly constant series, not a reason to remove the case or tune the scale floor.

The primary attribution contrast does not describe every selected institution. Pair 1 lacks a common 2026 cell. Pair 4 (source 281, target 267) has no complete three-seed paired primary scores; undefined oracle gaps are retained, and a flat prediction is not an informative ranking game. A stable mean on the informative complete panel cannot certify reliable explanations for the excluded low-volume regime.

## Gap and contribution

The defensible gap is an **empirical evaluation gap in institution-disjoint network deployment**: forecast error, exact allocation identities, accepted numerical integration and operator-response ranking are different forms of evidence, with different effective denominators. The study specifies these separately, applies the same forecasters to a separately frozen new target cohort, and explicitly tests timestamp density and uncertainty assumptions.

The contribution is a reproducible audit and evidence about its scope. Shapley, IG, cumulative perturbation responses, transfer forecasting and explanation generalization already exist. We do not claim the first OOD explanation method, a new attribution algorithm, causal demand truth, or cross-ISP validation. The original outcome informed the new protocol, which then preceded new-target scoring; the two estimates share sources/models and are not independent network replications.

## Is it worth submitting?

My assessment: **worth developing as a bounded empirical/measurement conference paper**, subject to venue fit and review. The expanded cohort improves the empirical basis, but a larger query count does not itself provide novelty. The story should emphasize what can and cannot be inferred, including non-recurrence of the original reference-specific secondary pattern, denominator exclusions and forecast-scale failures. A venue demanding a new model, a new explainer, a proved causal evaluation criterion or multiple independent networks would require further work.

The working title remains **Cross-Institution Reliability of Temporal Attributions in Network Traffic Forecasting**. It accurately states the evaluated subject without promising a degradation effect. The primary endpoint remains unchanged; no significant-looking sensitivity is promoted.

A distinct follow-up could validate conditional traffic replacements on an independently collected network, or study numerical/path behavior at clipping boundaries across multiple model families and physical units. Each needs its own question, evidence and protocol, rather than dividing this same benchmark into redundant papers.
