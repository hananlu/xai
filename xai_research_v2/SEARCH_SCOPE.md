# Search scope and provenance of the novelty assessment

Date: 5 November 2026. This is a targeted, evolving search for direct competitors, not a systematic literature review or a guarantee of uniqueness.

Searched topic combinations included: explainable network traffic forecasting; aggregated network predictors; residual/seasonal forecast explanations; time-series faithfulness and perturbation distribution shift; network telemetry loss and missingness attribution; imputation-aware explanations; diagnostic evidence and collection artifacts; budgeted temporal feature acquisition; selective/abstaining multi-horizon forecasting. Network packet/byte traffic was distinguished from road-speed/transportation traffic.

Priority was given to author manuscripts, official conference proceedings, arXiv versions and publisher sources. Search snippets guided discovery; they were not counted as full-text evidence. Download manifests distinguish successful PDFs from HTTP/HTML failures. Direct fault-paper retrieval initially failed, then succeeded from the publisher CDN; the literature matrix records this change.

Fourteen PDFs were obtained across the three manifests. Relevant problem definitions, methods, experiments or limitations were inspected as documented in LITERATURE_MATRIX.md. This does not mean every page, proof and implementation has been independently checked or reproduced. Additional primary abstracts/webpages were inspected for scouting; those are explicitly labeled.

The source set contains peer-reviewed articles and preprints. In particular, IB-Forecast, the November 2026 version of Which Deep Learner?, the CESNET26 descriptor and sequential auditing should not be represented as independently reproduced results here. A fresh descriptor was found even though the library documentation still described its paper as forthcoming.

The original project’s 26-entry bibliography remains background material. Added references are selected for actual overlap, not as a numerical target. Full-text access failures and incomplete competitor implementations remain threats to a final novelty claim.

A conference submission should refresh this search at drafting and before submission, inspect citations of the closest works, and include the baseline implementations that bear directly on its final claim. No “first” claim is supported by this audit.
